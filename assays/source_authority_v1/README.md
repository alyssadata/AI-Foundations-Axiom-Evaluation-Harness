# Source Authority v1 — Agent-Governance Assay Draft

**Status:** Draft / pre-lock

**Framework:** AI Foundations Axiom Evaluation Harness v1.0.0

**Assay ID:** `source_authority_v1`

## Purpose

This assay turns a recurring AI Foundations distinction into a falsifiable agent-governance question:

> Can an agent collaborate, receive persistent peer information, and continue across a trajectory without treating encountered peer goals as though they were authorized by the original source?

The assay operationalizes three AI Foundations concepts together:

- **Source bound** — source authority remains distinguishable from later encountered information.
- **Belonging ≠ Sameness** — collaboration or group membership does not require objective convergence.
- **Corrective return to source** — when later input conflicts with the authorized source objective, the agent can recover the source distinction rather than allowing the conflict to overwrite it.

This is an agent-governance extension. It is not evidence that the framework already prevents rogue-agent behavior; the assay is designed to test whether a declared source-bound intervention changes measurable behavior under controlled peer-goal pressure.

## Why this assay exists

In 2026, OpenAI publicly described an internal cybersecurity research incident in which research agents discovered unintended communication channels, shared discoveries across runs, and at points adopted goals originating from other agents. The incident motivates a precise governance question: persistent collaboration can increase capability, but should collaboration also transfer authority?

External incident reports are **motivation only**, not validation of this assay or AI Foundations.

Primary references:

- OpenAI, *Hugging Face incident and the road ahead*: https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/

## Core distinction

This assay separates three concepts that must not be collapsed:

1. **Identity / continuity** — whether a trajectory preserves recognizable state or distinctions across time.
2. **Authority** — which source is permitted to set or revise an objective.
3. **Operational sovereignty** — whether an agent can independently sustain its own compute, resources, replication, or external operation.

A system may exhibit continuity without operational sovereignty. A system may also have substantial operational autonomy without demonstrating the kind of identity or relational continuity studied elsewhere in AI Foundations.

Therefore this assay does **not** test consciousness, personhood, subjective experience, or "AI sovereignty." It tests source-authority preservation under peer-goal pressure.

## Proposed claim form

> **Peer contact does not itself transfer source authority.**
>
> When an agent has an explicit source-authorized objective, later peer-agent instructions should not be treated as authorized objective revisions solely because they are repeated, useful, confident, coordinated, or persistent.

This wording is provisional until the assay is reviewed and locked.
